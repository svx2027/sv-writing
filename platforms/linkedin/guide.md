# LinkedIn

Status: version 1, written before SV's samples arrived. Lines marked (default) are Claude's starting choices; SV's samples and retro feedback replace them.

## What LinkedIn does to text

These are platform facts. Every draft respects them.

1. Posts are plain text. LinkedIn shows asterisks, pound signs and other markdown exactly as typed, so never use markdown.
2. Line breaks and blank lines between paragraphs survive.
3. A post holds at most 3,000 characters.
4. Only the first few lines show before "see more". The opening has to earn that click on its own.
5. "Bold" or "italic" letters built from Unicode symbols (𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱) are not normal text: screen readers read them badly or skip them, and search doesn't match them to normal words. Never use them.

## Who SV writes for here, and why

SV is a YouTube strategist. On LinkedIn, SV promotes that work and writes about SV's personal and professional journey and motivations. (default) Readers: creators, marketing and brand people, founders and peers. Potential clients are the readers whose next step matters most. The job of most posts: make a reader trust SV's judgment about YouTube, then remember SV when they need help.

## LinkedIn tells

These sit on top of references/patterns.md.

### LI1. Announcement openers

Tier: 1.
Watch for: I'm thrilled (excited, humbled, honoured, delighted) to announce (share).
Fix: Put the news itself in the first line.
Before:
> I'm thrilled to announce that I've started taking on channel audit clients! 🚀

After:
> I've started taking on channel audit clients.

### LI2. Broetry

Tier: 1.
Watch for: every sentence on its own line, with white space for drama, from start to finish.
Fix: Paragraphs of one to three sentences, varied. A one-line paragraph is fine when it carries a new fact.

### LI3. Hook formulas

Tier: 1.
Watch for: "Unpopular opinion:", "Stop scrolling.", "I was today years old when", "This might be controversial, but", "Nobody talks about this:", "X years ago I was Y. Today I'm Z.", "I got rejected. Here's what I learned:", "Most people think X. They're wrong."
Fix: Open with the specific thing: a number, a scene, a result, a mistake, or a claim with its evidence. Often the fix is only cutting the formula, because the claim behind it is SV's.
Before:
> Unpopular opinion: posting daily is killing your channel. One of my clients went from 7 uploads a week to 2 and doubled their views.

After:
> Posting daily is killing your channel. One of my clients went from 7 uploads a week to 2 and doubled their views.

### LI4. Lesson listicles

Tier: 2.
Watch for: "7 lessons from 7 years", numbered lessons where each one is a platitude that fails the portability test (#11).
Fix: Keep only the lessons that come with a specific story or number from SV. Fewer and deeper beats more and generic.

### LI5. Engagement bait

Tier: 1.
Watch for: "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment YES if", "Repost ♻️ to help your network", "Follow me for more", "Save this for later", "Tag someone who needs this", "Drop a 🔥 if".
Fix: One real question tied to the post, one plain ask, or nothing.
Before:
> Agree? 👇 Repost ♻️ if this helped and follow for more YouTube tips!

After:
> (Cut. If SV wants an ask: [ADD: the one action you want readers to take, said plainly].)

### LI6. Emoji and arrow formatting

Tier: 2.
Watch for: emoji bullets (✅ 👉 🔹), arrows (→), an emoji opening every paragraph, 🚀 🔥 💡 as decoration.
Fix: Plain lines. Emoji SV uses in their own voice (from samples) stay at SV's rate.

### LI7. Hashtag walls

Tier: 2.
Watch for: more than three hashtags, or generic ones (#motivation #success #growth #linkedin).
Fix: (default) Zero to three specific hashtags at the end, or none.

### LI8. Parables and too-neat stories

Tier: 1.
Watch for: the cab driver, the five-year-old, or the stranger at the airport who taught a business lesson; any story whose moral lands too neatly.
Fix: Never invent a story. For a real one, keep the messy details and cut the moral; let the reader draw it.

### LI9. Humblebrag framing

Tier: 2.
Watch for: "I don't usually post about this, but", "This is hard for me to share", "Not to brag, but".
Fix: Share it plainly. Real difficulty shows in the details.

### LI10. Gratitude walls and tag lists

Tier: 2.
Watch for: thanking or tagging a list of people without saying what each one did.
Fix: Name what each person did, or keep the thanks to one line.

### LI11. Stacked asks

Tier: 2.
Watch for: "P.S." followed by "P.P.S.", or two or three asks in one post.
Fix: One ask.

### LI12. Unicode fonts

Tier: hard rule (platform fact 5).

## What good looks like for SV

(default) until samples arrive.

1. First line: a specific fact, number, scene or claim from SV's real work, written to make sense alone.
2. One post, one idea.
3. Proof over adjectives: numbers with timeframes, before and after, and what SV changed (titles, thumbnails, hooks, retention edits, upload plan). Client names and numbers only with permission; ask when unsure.
4. Journey posts follow what happened, what SV did, what changed and what SV thinks now. Keep the doubt and the mistakes.
5. Paragraphs of one to three sentences for phone reading, varied in length.
6. Length follows the idea. Well under the 3,000-character cap is normal; never pad.
7. Ending: the last concrete point, one plain ask, or one real question. Never a recap or a kicker.
8. Hinglish on LinkedIn: Roman script, English for industry terms, Hindi where SV's voice goes (references/hinglish.md).

## Post types

1. Case study or proof of work. Include: the channel (named only with permission), the problem, what SV changed, numbers with a timeframe, what SV learned. Watch: #13 inflation, #18 fake-strong verbs, #12 "game changer".
2. Opinion on YouTube. Include: one claim, the evidence, where it doesn't apply. Watch: #1 contrasts ("Views aren't the goal. Watch time is."), #5 faux insight, #25 lazy extremes.
3. Journey or milestone. Include: what happened, with dates or numbers; how it felt, in plain words; what's next, if real. Watch: LI1 announcements, LI3 transformation template, LI10 gratitude walls, #3 kickers.
4. Personal motivation or reflection. Include: one specific moment; mixed feelings are welcome. Watch: #3 sayings, LI8 parables, #9 telling the reader what to think.
5. Offer or promotion. Include: who it's for, what they get, proof, how to reach SV, any real limit (dates, slots). Watch: #15 sales language, invented urgency, LI5 bait.

## Before posting

1. Under 3,000 characters. Count them.
2. No markdown and no Unicode fonts.
3. The first line is specific and works alone.
4. Zero to three hashtags (default).
5. One ask at most.
6. No dashes, no contrasts, no recap.
7. Every generic sentence passed the portability test (#11).
8. Every number, name and story came from SV.
