# Eval

Run these checks after drafting in Write and Edit. Answer each one pass or fail. Fix every fail, then run the checks again. When two checks pull against each other, follow "When rules conflict" in SKILL.md. Detect uses only section 6. Suggest uses section 7 for its score.

This merges no-ai-slop's pass/fail eval, humanizer's self-audit and stop-slop's score.

## 1. Facts

1. Nothing added: no fact, number, name, date, quote, client, result, source or story that SV or the source didn't give. An `[ADD: ...]` placeholder marks every gap.
2. Nothing lost: every claim in the original survives, unless a pattern required the cut and What changed says so.
3. Rankings, comparisons and "at the same time" claims survived any reshaping.

## 2. Hard rules

1. Zero em or en dashes and no double hyphens. Search the text for them. A short hyphen joining two linked ideas (spaced or not, mixed with commas) is allowed only in SV's posts and captions.
2. No not-X-but-Y in any language or shape: one sentence, split across two, or a negative list.
3. No chatbot residue and no recap ending.
4. Headings in sentence case, except SV's posts and captions, which follow voice/profile.md (all lowercase, bare-digit numbered lists). Anything SV will paste is plain text with no markdown symbols, and lists use colons, with no dash or bullet characters, outside those posts and captions.
5. Hinglish first-person verbs match voice/profile.md, or neutral forms are used.

## 3. Voice

1. SV would recognize it as their own: vocabulary, cadence, humour, bluntness, doubt and asides kept.
2. It is the minimum effective edit. Strong human sentences were left alone, and the cutting is proportional to the slop.
3. Hinglish stayed Hinglish and English stayed English, with one register, one script and consistent spellings.
4. It sounds natural read aloud to a sharp colleague.

## 4. Substance

1. The point leads when setup adds nothing. Personal setup that adds tension or character stays.
2. Every generic sentence passed the portability test, or was cut or made specific.
3. Facts, examples and consequences carry the emphasis. No labels tell the reader what to think.
4. Active voice with human subjects where that is clearer, and direct verbs.

## 5. Platform

1. Every check in the platform guide's "Before posting" list passes.

## 6. Survivors

Search the final text once more for the tells that most often survive a rewrite: contrasts (#1), closers and fragments (#2), kickers (#3), run-ups (#4), faux insight (#5), self-answered questions (#6), colon reveals (#8), triads (#19), dashes (#21), bold or labelled lists (#29), and their Hinglish forms. In Detect, this search is the whole job.

## 7. Score

Rate each dimension from 1 to 10: 9 to 10 means nothing to fix, 7 to 8 minor fixes, 4 to 6 clear problems, 1 to 3 the problem runs through the whole piece.

1. Directness: statements, or announcements?
2. Rhythm: varied, or metronomic?
3. Trust: does it respect the reader's intelligence?
4. Authenticity: does it sound like SV? (Before samples exist: does it sound like a person?)
5. Density: is anything left to cut?

Gate: 35 out of 50, or 40 in strict mode. Below the gate, revise. If the score can only rise with facts SV hasn't given, stop revising, return the draft with `[ADD: ...]` placeholders, and say what is missing.
