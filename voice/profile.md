## Posts and captions style

SV's own style guide for LinkedIn posts and captions, given 2026-10-05. It applies to any post or caption SV publishes. The numbers rule overrides every other rule in this file.

Case
1: Everything lowercase. "I" is always capitalised. (2026-10-05)
2: The only other caps: one word for comic emphasis, once per post (CLEARLY). (2026-10-05)
3: Brand and tool names stay lowercase: claude, github, claude code. (2026-10-05)
4: Abbreviations stay as SV writes them, never expanded: rs., hrs, imp, bcoz, rn, ofc, wdn't, hv, f**in. (2026-10-05)

Punctuation
1: No full stops at the end of lines. Line breaks replace full stops. (2026-10-05)
2: Two linked ideas on one line are joined by a short hyphen or a comma, used interchangeably: "think in systems - systems give you freedom", "if they like it, they like the post". Mix the two across a post; never put a hyphen on every line. Spacing around the hyphen is not consistent either: sometimes spaced, sometimes not, so never force one uniform pattern. (2026-10-05)
3: Keep only what the meaning needs: commas inside numbers (86,700), decimal points (0.5%), apostrophes inside SV's abbreviations (wdn't), hyphens in compound words (token-efficient, non-engineer). (2026-10-05)
4: No em dashes, no semicolons, no quote marks, no double dots. (2026-10-05)

Sentences
1: One idea per line. Short lines, usually under 12 words. (2026-10-05)
2: A long thought becomes two lines, the second finishing the first: "speed is everything" then "you can perfect it later". (2026-10-05)
3: Casual words SV says stay: kinda, ofc, rn, so f**in cool, no idea how rn. (2026-10-05)
4: Vague or hedged phrases stay when that is how SV talks: "because of how things move sometimes". (2026-10-05)

Structure
1: Hook first, with numbers and value up front (money, hours, tokens, days). (2026-10-05)
2: Numbered lists use a bare digit and a space, no dot or colon: "1 think in systems". List markers stay digits even under 10. (2026-10-05)
3: Section headers are plain lowercase lines: "three things I learned". (2026-10-05)
4: Call back to an earlier point by its list number: "hence point 2". (2026-10-05)
5: An honest aside near the end: "about those 99 days". (2026-10-05)
6: A soft opt-out before extra detail: "skip if you like - but here is everything the carousel covers". (2026-10-05)

Tone
1: Personal, like talking to a friend at work. (2026-10-05)
2: Self-aware and a little self-deprecating (getting more token-efficient / CLEARLY). (2026-10-05)
3: Gratitude by name, kept simple: "thank you". (2026-10-05)
4: Confident about results, honest about what SV doesn't know yet. (2026-10-05)
5: No corporate words, no hype words, no hashtag stuffing. (2026-10-05)

Numbers (AP style; overrides everything else)
1: Spell out one to nine (three things, five trailers); numerals from 10 up (33 systems, 99 active days, 100s of hrs). (2026-10-05)
2: Always numerals, even below 10, for ages (a 5-year-old), percentages (5%, 0.5%), money (rs. 5, rs. 86,700+), dates and times (oct 4, 9 am), measurements (6 ft, 4 km), scores and votes (won 3-1) and large rounded numbers (25.1 billion tokens, 2 million views). (2026-10-05)
3: A number that starts a line is spelled out, except years: "thirty-three systems later". (2026-10-05)
4: List markers and point references stay digits: "1 think in systems", "hence point 2". (2026-10-05)
